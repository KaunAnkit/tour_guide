### What the artifact depicts
A recreated scene of the **signing of the Constitution of India**, showing a group
of statues in period dress gathered around a long table, bent over a large bound
volume (the Constitution), pen in hand, in the act of signing. The figures wear a
mix of formal Western suits, sherwanis, Gandhi caps, and sarees — reflecting the
diverse membership of the Constituent Assembly. The backdrop is an enlarged
facsimile newspaper clipping reporting on the Constitution, used as a scenic panel
rather than as a separate exhibit item.

### Historical event being represented
The Constitution of India was adopted by the Constituent Assembly on
**26 November 1949** and came into force on **26 January 1950** (Republic Day).
Between adoption and enforcement, members of the Constituent Assembly signed the
final handwritten, calligraphed copies of the Constitution over several days in
January 1950 — this is the moment such tableaux typically represent: dignitaries
and Assembly members putting their signatures to the founding document of the
Republic. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee of the Constituent
Assembly and is regarded as the chief architect of the Constitution, which is why
this scene is central to a memorial dedicated to him.

### Why this exhibit exists here
DANM is built in the shape of an open book, a direct reference to the Constitution.
The ground floor is dedicated to the story of how the Constitution came into being,
including replicas of pages of the original handwritten Constitution. This
signing tableau is one of the ground-floor exhibits (the museum has ~17–27 numbered
exhibits in total, each independently QR/app-tagged) that walks visitors through
that process, immediately after panels explaining the drafting and adoption.

### Suggested visitor-facing summary (for the LLM to draw on)
"This tableau recreates the historic moment when members of India's Constituent
Assembly signed the Constitution of India in January 1950, shortly before it came
into force on 26 January 1950 — the day India became a republic. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar,
as Chairman of the Drafting Committee, is remembered as the chief architect of this
document, which is why this scene has a central place in his memorial."
