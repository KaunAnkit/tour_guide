## What is visible in this image

The photograph depicts **Jawaharlal Nehru**, India's first Prime Minister, in the act
of signing the Constitution of India. He is seated at or leaning over a large ceremonial
table, pen in hand, appending his signature to one of the handwritten, calligraphed
volumes of the Constitution. Nehru is dressed in formal attire consistent with the
period — typically a white Nehru jacket (achkan) and white cap.

The setting is the **Central Hall of Parliament, New Delhi** (then referred to as the
Constituent Assembly Hall), the chamber where the Constituent Assembly conducted its
sessions from 1946 to 1950. The hall is characterised by its circular architecture,
high ceilings, and tiered gallery seating. In the background, other members of the
Constituent Assembly and invited dignitaries are visible, gathered to witness the
signing ceremony. Figures present likely include senior political leaders, jurists,
and Assembly members, some in sherwanis or Gandhi caps, others in sarees or Western
formal dress — reflecting the diverse composition of the Assembly.

The large bound volumes of the Constitution are visible on the table. The original
Constitution of India was handwritten and calligraphed in two scripts (Devanagari and
English) on parchment sheets by calligraphist Prem Behari Narain Raizada and
decorated with illustrations by artists from Shantiniketan. The bound volumes represent
the final, signed copy that became the founding document of the Republic.

## Historical context of the moment depicted

- The **Constituent Assembly adopted** the Constitution on **26 November 1949**
  (celebrated annually as Constitution Day / Samvidhan Diwas).
- Between late November 1949 and January 1950, Assembly members were invited to sign
  the final calligraphed copies of the Constitution in formal signing sessions.
- The **ceremonial signing was completed on 24 January 1950** — two days before the
  Constitution came into force.
- The Constitution **came into force on 26 January 1950**, the date India became a
  sovereign democratic republic, now celebrated as **Republic Day**.
- Jawaharlal Nehru's signature on the Constitution carries particular symbolic weight
  as the head of the first government of independent India and as the political leader
  who had championed the Constituent Assembly process alongside Ambedkar.

---

## Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's role in the Constitution

Although this photograph specifically documents Nehru's signing, the broader signing
ceremony and the Constitution itself are inseparable from **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar**, who:
- Served as **Chairman of the Drafting Committee** of the Constituent Assembly.
- Is widely regarded as the **chief architect of the Constitution of India**.
- Presented the final draft Constitution to the Assembly and guided it through debate
  and revision over approximately two years and eleven months of deliberation.

The Constitution Signing exhibit at DANM is installed in the memorial dedicated to
Ambedkar's life and legacy, making Nehru's signing — as one of the most prominent
Assembly signatories — a visible emblem of the collective achievement Ambedkar led
as Drafting Committee Chairman.
