## Visual Description

The image depicts pages from the **original Constitution of India** — the handwritten, calligraphed manuscript adopted by the Constituent Assembly on **26 November 1949** and brought into force on **26 January 1950**.

### Physical Appearance of the Document

The pages have a warm **cream or parchment tone**, consistent with the high-quality handmade paper (Parchment paper, produced at the Survey of India press in Dehradun) on which the Constitution was inscribed. The overall aesthetic is that of an **illuminated manuscript**: each page combines formal calligraphic text with richly decorated borders, giving the document the visual character of a state treasure rather than an administrative record.

### Calligraphic Script

The body text is rendered in a precise, formal calligraphic hand. The English text of the Constitution was calligraphed by **Prem Behari Narain Raizada** in a flowing italic script, working without any payment as a personal tribute to the nation. Each letter is uniform and carefully spaced, and the work fills the page from margin to margin in measured columns or paragraphs, depending on the section depicted.

### Decorative Borders and Illuminations

Each page is framed by **ornate decorative borders** featuring motifs drawn from Indian art, history, and culture. These borders were designed and painted by **Nandalal Bose**, the eminent artist and principal of Santiniketan's Kala Bhavan, along with his students and other contributing artists. The border motifs vary page by page to reflect the thematic content of each section:

- Images of the **Mohenjo-daro seal** (Indus Valley Civilisation) appear near the Preamble, anchoring the Constitution in India's deep historical roots.
- Scenes from the **Vedic period**, **Ramayana**, **Mahabharata**, and **Buddhist and Jain traditions** appear across successive pages.
- Panels depicting **Mughal and Maratha** architectural and cultural imagery represent the medieval period.
- **Motifs referencing the freedom struggle** and images of **Mahatma Gandhi's salt march** appear in later sections.
- **National symbols** — the lotus, elephants, lions — recur as framing devices throughout.

The border artwork is executed in warm golds, deep ochres, terracotta reds, and muted greens, giving the page a rich, polychrome character against the parchment ground.

### The Preamble

The **Preamble to the Constitution of India** is the most visually iconic page. It opens with the words:

> "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a SOVEREIGN DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC and to secure to all its citizens: JUSTICE, social, economic and political; LIBERTY of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship; EQUALITY of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all FRATERNITY assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity of the Nation…"

*(Note: the original 1949 text did not include the words "SOCIALIST" and "SECULAR", which were added by the 42nd Constitutional Amendment in 1976.)*

The Preamble page is decorated with an especially elaborate border by Nandalal Bose. The key words — **SOVEREIGN, DEMOCRATIC, REPUBLIC, JUSTICE, LIBERTY, EQUALITY, FRATERNITY** — are rendered in larger, capitalised lettering, giving the page a hierarchical typographic structure that visually emphasises the founding values of the republic.

## Historical and Curatorial Significance

The Constitution of India is one of the longest written constitutions of any sovereign nation. Its original manuscript comprises **470 Articles** organised into 22 Parts, along with 8 Schedules, all hand-calligraphed across **251 pages**. The completed manuscript was signed by members of the Constituent Assembly on **24 January 1950**, two days before it came into force.

**Dr. B.R. Ambedkar** served as the Chairman of the Drafting Committee of the Constituent Assembly and is widely regarded as the principal architect of the Constitution. The Drafting Committee was appointed on **29 August 1947** and submitted the final draft after approximately three years of deliberation.

The original handwritten Constitution is preserved in a **helium-filled case** in the Library of the Parliament of India, New Delhi. What is displayed at the Dr. B.R. Ambedkar National Memorial — and depicted in this image — is a **high-fidelity facsimile reproduction**, produced to allow visitors to see the calligraphic and artistic character of the document in an accessible museum setting.

**26 November** is observed as **Constitution Day** (Samvidhan Divas) in India, commemorating the date of its adoption by the Constituent Assembly in 1949.
